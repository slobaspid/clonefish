### The three things that matter

1. **Everything we have is an opening layer.** Post-ply-10 the *entire* stack is **+0.55 pp**.
   Openings: +11.4 pp. Early-mid/mid/end: indistinguishable from zero.
2. **The midgame null is real, not a harness bug.** I suspected it (identical means to 2 dp), and
   checked: `pooled` *does* change midgame predictions for **17/17** players (max per-player
   |Δtop-1| = 2.2e-2, mid Δnll = −0.0028). The per-player changes simply **cancel to +0.001 pp**.
   So the residual is applied everywhere and genuinely buys nothing in the midgame.
3. **Stacking is sub-additive.** book +2.91 and code +2.44 give +3.49 together, not +5.35 — they are
   largely reading the same (opening) signal. ~+1 pp over the better single layer.

### Temperature is not a model component
`base+T` and `pooled+T` are *identical* to their untempered versions in top-1 at every region
(temperature cannot move an argmax). It only moves NLL — and in the midgame the globally-fitted T
makes NLL slightly **worse** (+0.0025). Supports the design's rejection of fitted T as a component.

## The in-head vs stranger gap is CONFOUNDED (do not cite it as-is)

## Feasibility note for a 3,000-player shared head
`build_pooled_clone.train_shared` needs a **fat** cache per player: `(pooled, base_logits_over_legal,
legal_idx, legal_n, action)`. `sweep_cache_120_full.pt` is 2.2 GB for 120 players ≈ **18.7 MB/player**
→ 3,000 players ≈ **56 GB**. Infeasible locally as-is.
`scripts/build_cache_scale.py` builds only a **slim** cache (pooled + think + ids) — insufficient
to train the head.
**Implication:** a scale head needs a fat cache capped at ~150 games/player (3,000 × 150 ≈ the same
position count as 120 × 1,000, but 25× the user diversity — which is the stated bottleneck in
P2P 2510.16282: "training user diversity is more critical than sheer quantity").

## `[POP]` RESOLVED — the book gain is genuinely personal (n = 20)

Control: is the book gain *personal*, or just Lichess opening habits the chess.com-trained base
lacks? Cohort book = the *other* players' own moves, restricted to games dated before the target's
first val game (no look-ahead). Mean vs base:

| arm | Δtop-1 | Δnll |
|---|---|---|
| cohort book only | **+0.63 pp** | −0.0237 |
| personal book only | **+3.61 pp** | −0.1140 |
| cohort + personal (hierarchical) | +3.66 pp | −0.1160 |

**The personal book is ~6× the cohort book, and the cohort tier adds +0.05 pp on top of personal.**
So (a) L1 survives its control — this is identity, not a domain gap, and (b) **drop the hierarchical
cohort tier**: it is complexity for +0.05 pp. Ship the personal book alone.

## `[INHEAD]` — the in-head advantage does not replicate (6/12 done)

Same pool, same harness, same splits; the only variable is shared-head membership.
In-head pooled Δtop-1: +2.3, +3.0, +1.5, +4.7, +1.2, +3.3 → **mean ≈ +2.67 pp**, against the

## `[M0]` RESOLVED 2026-09-12 — the post-opening gain is IDENTITY, not domain adaptation

Full fine-tune of the base on **exactly 5,000 games** (`finetune_clone.py --max-train-games 5000`,
date-sorted, test = newest 40 games), then the same fine-tuned model evaluated on a **different**
player in the same rating band:

| | opening | early-mid | mid | end | **post-opening (own ply≥12)** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| latebloomer, own held-out (Δ) | +42.9 | +3.6 | +1.3 | −0.3 | **52.75 → 54.44 = +1.70 pp** |
| chess711, cross-player control (Δ) | −10.2 | −5.5 | −5.4 | −6.6 | **57.09 → 51.32 = −5.78 pp** |

**Both players are Lichess and the base is chess.com-trained.** If the gain were domain adaptation,
chess711 would have improved too — instead it degraded by 5.78 pp. So the fine-tune learned
*latebloomer specifically*. **The premise of identity-conditioned personalization survives its
falsification test.**

Read the +42.9 pp opening figure with care: latebloomer is the known low-floor outlier (base opening
top-1 only 27.9 %, memory `opening-gain-is-player-headroom`), and a fine-tune partly re-learns the book
our count-based L1 already captures — so it is not additive evidence.

Two design consequences: (a) the post-opening prize is **real but small** (+1.70 pp at 5k games, vs
Maia-individual's +2.8 pp at 10k — the right direction for half the data); (b) a private full
fine-tune **specialises hard** (−5.78 pp on a stranger), which argues for buying the same gain with a
routing row over *shared* adapters (MHR geometry) rather than a private dense update.

### Replication 1 (VEGETAL) — gate confirmed, and it exposes a TEMPORAL-DRIFT confound

| | own held-out post-opening | cross-player control |
|---|---|---|
| latebloomer | 52.75 → 54.44 (**+1.70 pp**) | chess711 −5.78 pp |
| VEGETAL | 54.55 → 59.65 (**+5.10 pp**) | bigbadbo55 −1.36 pp |

VEGETAL by phase: opening +18.1, early-mid +5.9, **middlegame +5.5**, endgame +3.8. Both players show
the same signature (own player up, a same-band stranger down), and for VEGETAL there is **no domain
confound at all** — both it and its control are Lichess pulls from the same harvester.

**Leakage ruled out explicitly** (this project has been burned before): for both PGNs, 0 games with
missing dates, **0 duplicate game URLs, 0 duplicate (date,time,White,Black) keys, and train/test
overlap = 0** after the date sort.

**But the +5.5 pp middlegame contradicts our whole prior corpus, and the cause looks like drift:**

| | train span | gap train→test | own-Elo across train | test Elo |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| VEGETAL | 2017-11-01 .. 2019-03-18 | **0 days** | 1820 → 1828 (flat) | 1819 |
| latebloomer | 2018-02-25 .. **2025-07-01** | **189 days** | 1670 → **1797** (+130) | 1764 |

latebloomer's 5,000-game train slice spans **7+ years** and she improved ~130 Elo across it, then is
tested 6 months later — so her fine-tune is partly cloning a *younger, weaker* player. VEGETAL's train
and test are contiguous with a flat rating. This matches the lit (van Opheusden: per-person parameters
correlate only 0.53–0.58 **across sessions**) and it means **VEGETAL's condition is the
deployment-relevant one** — a real user uploads games and plays immediately.

**Consequence worth taking seriously:** latebloomer has been this project's main full-fine-tune subject
and her history spans 9 years, so part of the standing "midgame is unpersonalizable" conclusion may be
a drift artifact. (It does *not* explain the residual/code null — that came from 1,200-game players.)

**Falsifiable prediction:** the three queued replications (Yespapa, bigbadbo55, OKENITE) are all
same-harvester pulls with contiguous spans and gap ≈ 0, so if drift is the driver they should land
near **+5 pp**, not +1.7. If they land near +1.7, drift is *not* the explanation and VEGETAL is simply
an unusually clonable player.

| player | train→test gap | post-opening Δ | cross-player control | opening | early-mid | mid | end |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| latebloomer | 189 d | **+1.70** | chess711 −5.78 | +42.9 | +3.6 | +1.3 | −0.3 |
| VEGETAL | 0 d | **+5.10** | bigbadbo55 −1.36 | +18.1 | +5.9 | +5.5 | +3.8 |
| Yespapa | 0 d | **+3.74** | kpowe52 −0.69 | +14.2 | +5.6 | +2.5 | +1.8 |
| bigbadbo55 (reverse-dir) | 0 d | **+3.09** | VEGETAL **+1.02** | +14.4 | +3.1 | +2.7 | +4.1 |
| OKENITE | 0 d | **+3.68** | MALOUMNJAK −3.56 | +37.9 | +4.8 | +4.4 | +2.3 |

**1. The drift prediction is only PARTIALLY supported.** Both gap-0 players (+5.10, +3.74) beat
latebloomer's +1.70, which is the predicted direction — but they differ from each other by 1.36 pp, so
**per-player variance is large and drift is not the whole story.** Do not attribute the spread to drift
until the within-player gap-control reports (same player, same 5,000-game count, only the gap moves).

**2. The consequential finding: a full fine-tune REACHES THE MIDDLEGAME; residuals do not.**
Per-player residuals/codes measured **+0.001 pp** midgame on 17 strangers. These full fine-tunes give
**+1.3 / +5.5 / +2.5** (mean ≈ +3.1). Same base, same players' data, same metric — the difference is
*where* the personalization is allowed to act. This is direct empirical support, on our own model, for
the central thesis of the architecture doc: **identity must condition the trunk, not ride on a frozen
one.** It also reframes the standing claim: "the middlegame is unpersonalizable" was a statement about
our *method*, not about chess.

**3. CORRECTION at n=4 — the cross-control is NOT reliably negative, and part of the raw gain IS
domain adaptation.** An earlier note in this file claimed all cross-controls were negative and that
therefore "none of these gains are domain adaptation". That was an overclaim. The four controls are
**−5.78, −1.36, −0.69, +1.02** — and the positive one is the *reverse direction of the same pair*
(ft-on-VEGETAL → bigbadbo55 = −1.36, but ft-on-bigbadbo55 → VEGETAL = **+1.02**), so the effect is
**asymmetric**, not uniformly protective.

**The upside: this finally gives a properly controlled identity estimate.** Both fine-tunes can be
compared on the *identical* eval set (VEGETAL's held-out games):

| model evaluated on VEGETAL's test games | post-opening top-1 |
|---|---|
| base | 54.55 |
| fine-tuned on **bigbadbo55** (a stranger) | 55.57 → **+1.02 = generic Lichess/domain adaptation** |
| fine-tuned on **VEGETAL** (self) | 59.65 → **+5.10 total, i.e. +4.08 identity-specific** |

This is a same-eval-set contrast, so it is a real control — unlike comparing Δ's measured on two
different players' test sets, which is what the earlier cross-control rows do.

**The reverse-direction pair gives the same contrast for bigbadbo55, for free** (its own run and
VEGETAL's run cross-evaluate each other), so there are now **two properly-controlled identity
estimates**:

| eval set | base | ft on a **stranger** | ft on **self** | **identity-specific** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| VEGETAL's test games | 54.55 | 55.57 (+1.02) | 59.65 (+5.10) | **+4.08 pp** |
| bigbadbo55's test games | 57.35 | 55.99 (−1.36) | 60.44 (+3.09) | **+4.45 pp** |

**Conclusion: the gate passes, and the well-controlled identity effect is ≈ +4.1 to +4.5 pp
post-opening at 5,000 games (mean ≈ +4.3) — larger than the +1.70 first reported and above
Maia-individual's +2.8 pp at 10,000 games.** Note the "domain adaptation" term is *not* a consistent
positive offset (+1.02 one way, −1.36 the other): training on a stranger sometimes helps slightly and
sometimes hurts. The robust quantity is the **self-minus-stranger difference on a fixed eval set**.
**Methodological rule: never quote a fine-tune gain without subtracting a stranger-fine-tune baseline
measured on the same eval set.** Two more reverse-direction runs (kpowe52→Yespapa,
MALOUMNJAK→OKENITE) are queued to take this from n=2 to n=4.

Still true: the most idiosyncratic player (latebloomer, biggest opening gain) does the most damage to a
stranger (−5.78), which is the expected pattern for a private dense weight update and the argument for
shared-adapter routing in Stage C.

**4. The midgame result holds at n=5:** +1.3 / +5.5 / +2.5 / +2.7 / +4.4 → **mean ≈ +3.3 pp**, against
**+0.001 pp** for residuals/codes on a frozen base. Same base, same players' data, same metric; the only
difference is whether personalization may touch the trunk. Still on probation pending independent
metrics (1-NN, MMD), but the method-vs-game reframing stands.

**5. Own-player post-opening gains, n=5.** The four gap-0 players give
**+5.10 / +3.74 / +3.09 / +3.68 → mean +3.90 pp** (spread 2.0 pp). latebloomer's +1.70 is the outlier.

**5b. THE DRIFT HYPOTHESIS IS REFUTED — it was mine, and the gap-control kills it.**
Within-player control: same player (VEGETAL), same 5,000-game count, **same 1,952-position test set**,
training shifted ~960 games (~3 months) further back in time.

| VEGETAL | post-opening | opening | early-mid | mid | end |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| normal (gap 0 d) | 54.55 → 59.65 = **+5.10** | +18.1 | +5.9 | +5.5 | +3.8 |
| gap-control (~3 mo) | 54.55 → 59.17 = **+4.62** | +17.3 | +5.3 | **+5.9** | +2.5 |

**A 3-month shift costs 0.48 pp.** That cannot explain the 2.2 pp gap between latebloomer (+1.70) and
the gap-0 mean (+3.90); the midgame is if anything *higher* in the shifted condition. So the spread
across players is **per-player variance, not temporal drift**, and the earlier claim in this file that
"VEGETAL's condition is the deployment-relevant one — measure with gap ≈ 0 or you understate the gain"
is **withdrawn**: recency of the training window barely matters over this horizon.

*Honest limit of this control:* it tested a ~3-month shift on a player whose whole history spans 17
months. latebloomer's condition is different in kind — a 7-year training span with +130 Elo gained
across it — so a large, multi-year drift is not excluded. What is excluded is drift as the explanation
for the observed spread.

*Practical upside:* personalization does **not** need the user's most recent games, which relaxes a
deployment constraint we might otherwise have imposed.

### Reproducibility: run-to-run noise is ~0.1 pp, so the between-player spread is real
VEGETAL was fine-tuned a second time from scratch on the identical configuration (5,000 games,
3 epochs) to produce a saveable model: **+5.23 pp vs +5.10 pp** post-opening. So **run-to-run variance
is ≈ 0.13 pp**, two orders of magnitude smaller than the **5 pp between-player spread**
(0.79 → 5.81). That licenses the headroom inference above: the spread is a property of the *players*,
not of the training process.

### PROBATION VERDICT (independent metrics, VEGETAL, n=1): WEAK PASS

The midgame/post-opening gain was placed on probation because it contradicts the cross-domain finding
that no field reliably clones *decisions*, and because this project already had a personalization gain
evaporate under exactly this check. Recognizer-space distribution metrics (never part of the training
objective), 120 real vs 120 self-play games:

| | MMD² (0 = identical clouds) | 1-NN same-class (0.5 = indistinguishable) |
|---|---|---|
| real-vs-real floor | −0.0003 | 0.5 (ideal) |
| BASE self-play | 0.0433 | 0.883 |
| **fine-tuned clone** | **0.0328** | **0.825** |
| gap closed | **24 %** | **15 %** |

**Verdict: passes, directionally, on both metrics independently.** The clone is measurably closer to the
real player than the base is, on a signal it was never trained against. Both arms are self-play, so
self-play drift largely cancels in the contrast.

**Why it is only a weak pass:** 1-NN remains **0.825 vs an ideal 0.5** — the clone is still easily
separable from the human. Personalization *moves* the distribution; it does not close it. And this is
**one player, one seed, no error bars**, so the 5 pp between-player variance seen in move-match is
unmeasured here. A kpowe52 replication is queued.

**Think-time shape** (real → clone, base for reference): mean 2.91 → **2.96** (base 3.22); std 3.13 →
**2.98** (base 3.74); snap% 7 → **6** (base 9); median 2.00 → 2.13 (base 1.90); p90 7.00 → 6.05
(base 7.39). The fine-tune is closer on 3 of 5 statistics, base on 2 — roughly a wash, consistent with
the standing finding that timing realism comes from *sampling* the time head rather than personalising it.

### Which number to quote
- **vs base (+3.41 mean)** is the *deployment-relevant* gain — base is what ships without personalization.
- **vs stranger-ft (+4.67 mean)** establishes that the gain is *identity*, not domain adaptation.
These answer different questions; do not conflate them (an earlier note here said to always quote the
stranger-subtracted figure — that was too strong).
- **Product consequence:** strong players (~1800+ with high base predictability) should be told to expect
little from cloning; weaker/less predictable players gain the most.

**6. Cross-controls, all five:** −5.78, −1.36, −0.69, **+1.02**, −3.56 (mean −2.07). Mostly protective
but *not* reliably so (see the correction in point 3), which is why the same-eval-set contrast — not the
cross-control Δ — is the number to quote.

## B1 RESOLVED 2026-09-12 - the midgame gain is NOT position memorisation

Book built from VEGETAL 5000 training games: **203,536 distinct positions** out of about 210,000 own
moves, i.e. after the opening a position essentially never repeats. Held-out post-opening positions
(own ply >= 12): n = 1472, evaluated with the saved fine-tuned model `clones/ft5k_VEGETAL.pt`.

| bucket | n | base | ft | delta |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| post-opening ALL | 1472 | 54.55 | 59.78 | **+5.23 pp** |
| book-SEEN (N>=1) | **0** | - | - | - |
| book-UNSEEN (N=0) | **1472** | 54.55 | 59.78 | **+5.23 pp** |

**Every single post-opening test position is book-unseen. Explanation (b), book or repeat leakage past
ply 12, is dead.** The gain is measured entirely on positions the player has never occupied, so it
cannot be position lookup. **Rung 2 of the ladder PASSES.**

**What this does NOT settle:** the fine-tune may be learning habits or traits (always plays h3, always
trades on c6) rather than identity-specific judgement. That is the (a) versus (c) question, and it
requires B2, the trait-matched cohort. B2 is now the critical experiment in the programme.

## B2 RESOLVED 2026-09-12 - it is a MIXTURE: ~20% generic, ~30% traits, ~50% identity

Target VEGETAL. All arms: 1160 training games, adapt=full, lr 1e-5, 3 epochs, Elo band +-75 of 1842,
evaluated on the SAME held-out 40 games (post-opening own ply >= 12). Self-reference from
`results/arch_bench/VEGETAL_full.log`.

| arm | post-opening | delta | share of total |
|---|---|---|---|
| base | 54.55 | - | - |
| trait-ANTI-matched cohort (5 players) | 55.50 | **+0.95** | 20% generic |
| trait-MATCHED cohort (5 players) | 56.93 | **+2.38** | +1.43 over anti = 30% traits |
| self (own games) | 59.31 | **+4.76** | +2.38 over matched = 50% identity |

Cohort selection: 53 candidates within +-75 Elo; ranked by z-scored distance on 8 identity-free traits
(median think time, snap rate, tank rate, capture rate, check rate, castle-by-move-10, first-move
entropy, own-ply count). Mean trait distance: matched 2.08, anti 5.95.

**Verdict on the central question.** The kill criterion was "if trait-matched strangers recover more
than 70% of the gain, drop identity conditioning". They recover **50%**, so **identity conditioning
survives** - but traits are worth a real **+1.43 pp** over a same-Elo generic cohort, and generic
same-Elo adaptation alone is worth +0.95 pp.

**Confound, stated plainly.** The matched-vs-anti contrast (+1.43) is CLEAN: identical pool size,
identical pooling structure, identical Elo band, only trait distance differs. The self-vs-matched gap
(+2.38) is NOT clean: it compares one player x 1160 games against five players x 232 games, so
"identity" is entangled with single-player concentration. Therefore: **traits carry at least 1.43 pp;
identity carries up to 2.38 pp.** To separate, run a 5-player cohort that INCLUDES the target, or a
self arm at 232 games.

**Power.** n=1 target against a paired sd of 1.21 pp. Both increments are of the same order as the
noise, so this is SUGGESTIVE, not established. Needs 3 or more targets, and only targets with real
headroom (MALOUMNJAK totals +0.47, so there is nothing to decompose there).

**The unlooked-for product finding: trait-matched cohorts are a COLD-START mechanism.** +2.38 pp on a
player whose own games were never used. A user with 20 games can be initialised from trait-similar
players and get about half the benefit before any personal training. This is Maia4All prototype
initialisation, measured on our own model and our own trait vector.
