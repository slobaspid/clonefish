| 1 | McIlroy-Young et al., *Learning Models of Individual Behavior in Chess*, KDD'22 (arXiv 2008.10086) | (fetched via WebFetch cache) | Full fine-tune of Maia 1900 per player. | 2 | Tang et al., *Learning to Imitate with Less* (Maia4All), arXiv 2507.21488 | lit/pdf/2507.21488.pdf | Individual modeling from **20 games** instead of 5,000 (250x). Two-stage: (a) fine-tune Maia-2 on diverse *prototype players* w/ rich histories to shift population->individual, (b) then fine-tune on the low-resource player. Also: solve a *discriminative* nearest-prototype task before the generative one. 51.4 -> 53.2%. **VERIFIED 2026-09-12 from the PDF:** they "disregard the initial 10 plies of each game" and positions under 30 s, so **all their numbers are POST-OPENING** — the exact region where our whole stack is worth +0.55 pp. Maia4All-Prototype beats Maia by **>2.5 p.p.** (perplexity ~1.2 better); **+1.9 p.p. from 800 positions (~20 games)** with prototype-informed init; prototype matching alone IDs the player at **89% from 1 shot of 1100 candidates**. The kill-shot for our design: **population-level fine-tuning of the base gains only 0.5 p.p.** — so their gain is IDENTITY, not domain adaptation. Mechanism: Maia-2 already modulates prediction through *population/skill embeddings*; enrichment **extends those to individual embeddings** and moves base weights population->individual. "fine-tuning directly on low-resource players doesn't work, but first enriching the model ... is key". | Direct fix for the data floor, and the strongest evidence that identity must condition the TRUNK, not a residual on a frozen base. See `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-12-clonefish-architecture.md`. | R |
| 3 | Omi, Caccia, Sarkar, Ash, Sen (MSR), *Generative Modeling of Individual Behavior at Scale*, arXiv 2502.14998 | lit/pdf/2502.14998.pdf | Bank of LoRA adapters + a routing matrix over a single shared BC model; each player = one row of the routing matrix = "style vector". Generative AND scalable AND compact (prior work was only two of three). Few-shot new players, stylometry at scale, plus algorithmic *style steering*. Also applied to Rocket League. **VERIFIED 2026-09-12 from the PDF:** chess config = shared inventory of **32 rank-16 LoRA adapters with 8-head routing** over 24 MHR layers; the adapters are POPULATION parameters and a player is only **their routing row** (a softmax mixture over shared latent "skills"). Fitting a new player is **routing-only fine-tuning**, and **"100 games is sufficient to learn the style vector of an unseen player"**. It lands **"within 1% accuracy of individual model fine-tuning ... at roughly 1% of the compute per player"** — so full fine-tuning is a COST choice, not an accuracy one. Scale: 47,864 players, stylometry 94.4% (unseen few-shot 87.6%); per-player move-match 45-69%. | The scalable alternative to one-model-per-player, and the right PARAMETER GEOMETRY for us: thousands of per-user weights, not the tens of thousands that made our v1 adapter regress -6.2 pp. Recommended Stage C mechanism. | R |

# Cross-field imports (math / physics / ML)
Added 2026-09-09. Organised by which project blocker they attack.

## A. Per-player residual barely helps; personalization LOSES below ~1k games
| Paper | Local | Idea to steal |
|---|---|---|
| Hierarchical Bayes / partial pooling (textbook method, not one paper) | — | Each player's parameters shrink toward the population mean **in proportion to how much data they have**. Automatically interpolates between "pure base" and "pure clone" — no hand-tuned residual scale. Maia-individual's -3.0pp at 1k games is textbook unpooled overfitting; this is the standard fix. |
| Yeung, *Deep-IRT*, arXiv 1904.11738 | lit/pdf/1904.11738.pdf | Two networks (examinee net + item net) keep IRT parameters interpretable inside a deep model. **Elo is already a Rasch/IRT model**; multidimensional IRT makes strength one axis and style the others, jointly estimated. That is the Elo-disentanglement problem with decades of estimation theory attached. |

## B. Think-time head plateaued (MDN over think seconds)
| Paper | Local | Idea to steal |
|---|---|---|
| Fengler, Govindarajan, Chen, Frank, *Likelihood Approximation Networks (LANs)*, eLife 2021 | lit/pdf/fengler_LANs.pdf | **The key enabler.** Train a NN to approximate the likelihood of a simulation model that has no closed form, then do fast posterior inference / backprop through it. This is what makes "put a drift-diffusion head on the transformer" actually trainable. Ships code for arbitrary *hierarchical* instantiations — composes with (A). |
| Echenique, Fallah, Jordan, *A General Framework for Estimating Preferences Using Response Time Data*, arXiv 2507.20403 | lit/pdf/2507.20403.pdf | Formal theory of what response time can and cannot identify about preferences. Relevant to our closed "timing carries identity" thesis — says precisely what RT is entitled to tell us. |
| Goetschalckx et al., *Human-like reaction time from stable recurrent vision models*, arXiv 2306.11582 | lit/pdf/2306.11582.pdf | Deriving an RT metric from a network's own dynamics rather than a separate regression head. |
| (background) DDM/sequential-sampling: think-time = first-passage time of a noisy accumulator to a threshold. Gives the right-skewed shape and the difficulty->slowdown coupling for free instead of learning them. | — | |

## C. Move accuracy is the wrong metric (UCLouvain used Jensen-Shannon)
| Paper | Local | Idea to steal |
|---|---|---|
| Luo et al., *Optimal Transport for Offline Imitation Learning*, ICLR 2023, arXiv 2303.13971 | lit/pdf/2303.13971.pdf | Wasserstein beats JSD here: it is a true metric AND defined for **non-overlapping supports** — two players who never pick the same move still get a meaningful distance. JSD saturates there. |
| Chang et al., *Imitation Learning from Observation through OT*, RLC 2024, arXiv 2310.01632 | lit/pdf/2310.01632.pdf | OT distance between learner and expert trajectory distributions used directly as the training signal, not just an eval metric. |
| Fickinger, Cohen, Russell, Amos, *Cross-Domain Imitation Learning via OT*, ICLR 2022, arXiv 2110.03684 | lit/pdf/2110.03684.pdf | Gromov-Wasserstein aligns behaviour across *different* state spaces. Speculative here, but it is the tool if we ever compare players across time controls or platforms. |

## D. Few-shot / new player without per-player fine-tuning
| Paper | Local | Idea to steal |
|---|---|---|
| Garnelo et al., *Conditional Neural Processes*, ICML 2018, arXiv 1807.01613 | lit/pdf/1807.01613.pdf | The right formalism for the whole clone problem: condition on a context set of K games, emit a predictive distribution. No per-player weights at all. Subsumes "clone from 20 games". |
| Patacchiola et al., *Comparing Fine-Tuning and Meta-Learning for Few-Shot Policy Imitation*, CoLLAs 2023, arXiv 2306.13554 | lit/pdf/2306.13554.pdf | **Counterweight — read before committing to meta-learning.** Finds plain pretrain-then-fine-tune is *competitive with* meta-learning for few-shot imitation, without the expensive meta-training protocol. Argues against jumping to a Maia4All-style scheme on principle alone. |
