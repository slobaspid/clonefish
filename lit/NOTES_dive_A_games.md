## Top 5 ideas from games AI for user-games → clone engine

1. **Fit the user's conditioning inputs by likelihood first: effective rating pair, temperature, and a time-speed scalar.** (KataGo profiles; Kuboki 2505.00279; psychometric per-person temperature.)
   - Expected gain: +0.5–1 pp top-1. Bigger gains on feel: strength, blunder rate, variety.
   - Data/compute: 10–20 games; seconds on CPU (1-D/3-D grid search on the user's games).
   - Risk: low. The rating input may absorb what the per-player code does.
2. **Search sized by predicted think time, anchored to the personalized policy; sample from the smoothed visit policy, never argmax.** (Allie adaptive + piKL.)
   - Expected gain: about +0.2–0.8 pp midgame move-match at 1500–1900 blitz (piKL App. D). Better strength calibration. Fewer long-think blunders, while fast moves stay raw and fallible.
   - Data/compute: no new data; 0–50 playouts of the 19.5M model per move on CPU. c and cpuct are tuned once.
   - Risk: medium. The value head's quality matters. The most-visited move costs 18.7 pp (2605.11893). Gains shrink at fast time controls.
3. **Treat the KL/cpuct anchor λ as the per-clone strength dial.** (DiL-piKL.)
   - Expected gain: the clone's Elo matches the player's rating without losing human-likeness. Replaces the weak Elo dial.
   - Data/compute: calibrate with self-play against the base model at several rating inputs; CPU-hours once, not per user.
   - Risk: medium. It is tied to #2.
4. **Hierarchical Dirichlet backoff beyond the exact book, plus Thompson-sampled persona per game.** Backoff chain: exact FEN → opening family / pawn skeleton → model. (Bayes' Bluff; Ganzfried.)
   - Expected gain: small top-1. Better repertoire realism and game-to-game variety.
   - Data/compute: counts only; milliseconds.
   - Risk: low. Beyond move 12 the counts are sparse, and ChessMimic found its database had "negligible" top-1 impact.
5. **Couple think time to value-of-computation (policy entropy / value gap) and judge clones on distribution metrics.** Metrics: blunder rate by position difficulty and clock, move-distribution divergence against the same-player vs different-player gap. (Russek; Anderson; Sunde; 2605.11893.)
   - Expected gain: feel only (fixes Allie's "long think on forced move" defect). Stops top-1 driving decisions.
   - Data/compute: CPU, from existing heads.
   - Risk: low. The metric may disagree with human judgement (Allie's feedback vs its metrics).

## Three decision-changing facts

1. **Search is not a big midgame accuracy lever at blitz.** Best case +1.1 pp (Maia1900, excluding moves with <30 s left). Only +0.2 pp for 1500 players at the shortest time controls. Most-visited MCTS costs 18.7 pp. Use search for strength calibration and feel, not move-match.
2. **Blunders track position difficulty more than the clock.** Longer thinks mean more blunders, not fewer. So realistic mistakes by clock come from a calibrated policy plus an entropy-coupled time head, not a time-pressure noise knob.
3. **No game-AI system models individual think time or offers per-player profiles.** KataGo HumanSL conditions only on rank, era, and the rank pair. Meanwhile 10–20 games pin a player's skill level with 80–92% accuracy. Per-user likelihood fitting of the conditioning inputs is the cheapest, best-supported first step.
