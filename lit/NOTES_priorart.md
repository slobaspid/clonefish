## Novelty verdicts

1. **Exact per-player opening book + Dirichlet blend with neural human-move model: PARTIAL PRIOR ART.**
   - Closest: ChessMimic's per-rating-band empirical move database. It uses hard lookup with fallback, is keyed on FEN plus the last 12 moves, and reports "negligible impact on top-1".
   - Maia-individual (2021 version) saw that the opening/seen-position effect exists. It chose to model unseen positions instead and never built a per-player book.
   - Not found: a per-PLAYER count table smoothed with a neural prior. Also not found: any claim that personal books account for most personalization gains. The Maia-2021 evidence points the other way: gains persist in unseen, late-game positions.
   - The smoothing math is standard (cache LM / Dirichlet LM, already in corpus). The novelty would be applying it in chess, not the method itself.

2. **Per-player temperature fit on held-out likelihood + sampling: PARTIAL PRIOR ART.**
   - A per-person softmax inverse temperature as an individual trait is textbook in cognitive and psychometric modelling (2605.09305, citing LaMar 2018).
   - Sampling for human-likeness is standard in chess engines (Allie; ChessMimic samples from its database).
   - Not found: per-player temperature on a Maia-style model, or evidence it matches blunder rate or variety. Guo-style temperature scaling fit per user is a small, obvious step, not a contribution.

3. **Stylometry-encoder nearest-neighbour "style twins" to borrow data or counts: DONE BEFORE in NLP; PARTIAL in chess.**
   - Welch et al. (ACL 2022) do almost exactly this: an authorship-attribution similarity picks anchor users, then data is borrowed or LMs interpolated. Authorship-attribution similarity was best for interpolation.
   - Their gains were small (about 1 perplexity point). Returns plateau past about 10 neighbours, and naive similar-user fine-tuning sometimes did not help.
   - Chess-side: Maia4All prototypes (parameter-space, not count borrowing).
   - Not found: hierarchical Dirichlet over user counts, then twin counts, then model, in chess.

4. **Stylometry encoder as clone-evaluation metric: DONE BEFORE, with well-documented pitfalls.**
   - Standard in voice cloning (SECS/EER, SV2TTS lineage) and in text style imitation (Patel 2022; Wang 2025).
   - Documented pitfalls:
     - (a) Circularity: evaluate with an encoder not used in training or selection (SIG-VC).
     - (b) The metric disagrees with human similarity ratings and can exceed ground truth (YourTTS).
     - (c) ID encoders capture static, easy cues and miss dynamic behaviour; nuisance factors shift scores (Carbonneau 2025).
     - (d) Raw scores must be calibrated against genuine same-person pairs.
   - Using it for chess clones would be new, but only as an application.
